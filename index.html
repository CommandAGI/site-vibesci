A chemistry lab you run from a prompt

Before you touch the reagents, run the protocol.

VibeSci reads a written liquid-handling protocol, builds a digital twin of your bench, and runs it. When a well comes out wrong it names the step that failed it — a tip that was not ejected, a mix that never happened, a volume written wrong — then rewrites the protocol and re-runs it to prove the fix.

Run a protocol in the twin What it does

The demo runs in your browser, on a model of a bench. It sends nothing and calls no model. It is not a substitute for your lab's own validation.

Four steps, one loop

  1. Give it the protocol

    A protocol as your lab writes it: the transfers, the volumes, the mixes, the tips. Paste it or describe it in a sentence.

  2. It runs on a twin of the bench

    Wells hold layers, a tip keeps a film, a reservoir never runs out. The arithmetic is the liquid's, not a score.

  3. It shows where it fails

    The first step and the well: target against what the twin found, and the cause in the bench's words.

  4. It rewrites and proves it

    Every line it adds says why. The rewritten protocol runs in the same twin, and the table has to come back green.

What it catches

Carryover

A tip that still holds the last solution puts a film of it into the next well. On a blank, that film is the result.

A missed mix

An unmixed well is a bolus on top of diluent. The next aspirate takes the bolus, and the series walks away from its own numbers.

A wrong volume

Twelve microlitres where ten were written is a dilution factor that is no longer the one on the page.

A target nothing meets

A series can be internally consistent and still miss the concentration the assay needs. Every well is compared with the target, not with its neighbour.

Where it is going

Today the lab reads a protocol and a target per well, and runs both on the twin. Next: a bench photo becomes the twin's geometry, a model reads the protocol out of a PDF, and the twin runs in 3D beside the plate.
